1. Introduction to Cell

  • A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
  • All living organisms are made up of cells.
  • Unicellular: Organisms made up of one cell.
  • Multicellular: Organisms made up of many cells.
  • Examples: Amoeba and bacteria are unicellular; humans and plants are multicellular.
  • Cells perform nutrition, respiration, growth, excretion and reproduction.

2. Discovery of Cell

  • Robert Hooke (1665): Discovered cells in cork.
  • Antonie van Leeuwenhoek: Observed living cells.
  • Matthias Schleiden: Plants are made of cells.
  • Theodor Schwann: Animals are made of cells.
  • Rudolf Virchow: New cells arise from pre-existing cells.

3. Cell Theory

  • All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
  • The cell is the basic unit of structure and function.
  • All cells arise from pre-existing cells.

4. Types of Cells

  • Prokaryotic Cells: Lack a true membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
  • Example: Bacteria.
  • Eukaryotic Cells: Have a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
  • Examples: Plant, animal, fungal and protist cells.

5. Main Parts of a Cell

  • Plasma Membrane: Controls movement of substances into and out of the cell.
  • Cytoplasm: Jelly-like material where many cell activities occur.
  • Nucleus: Contains DNA and controls cell activities.
  • Cell Wall: Rigid outer covering in plant cells, mainly made of cellulose. Provides support and protection.

6. Cell Organelles and Their Functions

  • Mitochondria: Powerhouse of the cell; releases usable energy from food.
  • Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER): Rough ER helps in protein processing; Smooth ER helps make lipids and in detoxification.
  • Ribosomes: Sites of protein synthesis.
  • Golgi Apparatus: Modifies, sorts and packages proteins and lipids.
  • Lysosomes: Break down waste and worn-out cell parts.
  • Vacuoles: Storage sacs; plant cells usually have one large central vacuole.
  • Plastids: Found in plants and algae. Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll and perform photosynthesis.

7. Plant Cell vs Animal Cell

Plant Cell:

  • Has a cell wall.
  • Has plastids.
  • Usually has a large central vacuole.

Animal Cell:

  • Does not have a cell wall.
  • Does not have plastids.
  • Usually has smaller vacuoles.

Both: Plasma membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria, ER, Golgi apparatus and ribosomes.

8. Movement of Substances

  • Diffusion: Movement of particles from higher concentration to lower concentration.
  • Osmosis: Movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from higher water concentration to lower water concentration.
  • Plasmolysis: Shrinkage of cell contents away from the cell wall when a plant cell loses water in a concentrated solution.

9. Important Differences

Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic

  • Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus.
  • Eukaryotic cells have a membrane-bound nucleus.

Cell Wall vs Plasma Membrane

  • Cell wall: Rigid and freely permeable in many contexts.
  • Plasma membrane: Flexible and selectively permeable.

Diffusion vs Osmosis

  • Diffusion: Movement of particles.
  • Osmosis: Movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane.

10. Quick Revision – Key Terms

  • Cell: Basic unit of life.
  • Organelle: Specialised structure inside a cell.
  • DNA: Hereditary material.
  • Chlorophyll: Green pigment that absorbs light for photosynthesis.
  • Cellular Respiration: Process that releases energy from food.
  • Selectively Permeable: Allows some substances to pass more easily than others.
