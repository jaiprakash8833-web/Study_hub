REPRODUCTION: HOW LIFE CONTINUES

Class 9 Science • Quick Revision Notes

Definitions Key Differences Examples Practice

1. What is Reproduction?

Reproduction is the biological process by which living organisms produce new individuals of their own kind. It ensures continuity of a species from one generation to the next.

Why is reproduction important?

  • It prevents a species from disappearing.
  • It transfers hereditary information (DNA) from parents to offspring.
  • It produces variations that may help populations adapt to changing environments over generations.

2. Types of Reproduction

Asexual reproduction

Usually involves one parent. There is no fusion of male and female gametes. Offspring are usually genetically very similar to the parent.

Sexual reproduction

Usually involves male and female gametes and their fusion (fertilisation). It generally combines genetic information from two parents and produces variation.

3. Methods of Asexual Reproduction

Method Main feature Example
Binary fission One parent cell divides into two daughter cells. In Amoeba, the nucleus divides first, followed by cytoplasm. Amoeba, many bacteria
Multiple fission Nucleus divides repeatedly and many daughter cells form at the same time. Plasmodium
Budding A small outgrowth (bud) develops on the parent and may separate. Yeast, Hydra
Fragmentation Body breaks into pieces; each suitable fragment can grow into a new individual. Spirogyra
Regeneration A complete individual can develop from a body fragment in some organisms. Planaria
Spore formation Tiny spores can survive unfavourable conditions and grow when conditions become suitable. Rhizopus (bread mould)
Vegetative propagation New plants grow from roots, stems or leaves instead of seeds. Potato, ginger, Bryophyllum, sugarcane
Remember: Regeneration is not the usual method of reproduction in most complex animals.

Advantages of vegetative propagation

  • Useful for plants that do not produce viable seeds easily.
  • New plants may mature and bear flowers or fruits sooner than seed-grown plants.
  • Desirable features of the parent plant are usually retained.

4. Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

A flower is the reproductive structure of many plants.

Part Function
Sepals Protect the flower bud.
Petals Often attract pollinators.
Stamen (male part) Anther produces pollen grains; filament supports the anther.
Pistil/Carpel (female part) Stigma receives pollen; style connects stigma to ovary; ovary contains ovules.

Pollination

Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a flower.

  • Self-pollination: within the same flower or plant.
  • Cross-pollination: between flowers on different plants of the same species.
  • Agents include insects, wind, water and other animals.

Fertilisation and seed formation

After pollination, a pollen tube may grow through the style towards an ovule. The male gamete travels through it and fuses with the female gamete in the ovule. This is called fertilisation.

  • The fertilised egg forms an embryo.
  • The ovule develops into a seed.
  • The ovary usually develops into a fruit.
Pollination → Pollen tube growth → Fertilisation → Embryo formation → Seed and fruit development

5. Sexual Reproduction in Humans (Basic Overview)

  • Testes produce sperm cells.
  • Ovaries produce egg cells (ova).
  • Fertilisation normally occurs in the oviduct (fallopian tube), where a sperm may fuse with an egg to form a zygote.
  • The zygote divides repeatedly and develops into an embryo.
  • The embryo becomes implanted in the lining of the uterus and receives oxygen and nutrients through the placenta as it develops.
  • After the period of development, the baby is born.

7. Why Does Sexual Reproduction Create Variation?

During sexual reproduction, genetic material from two gametes combines. The offspring receives a new combination of hereditary information. Therefore, individuals of the same species are not exactly alike. Variation provides raw material for evolution and may help some individuals survive environmental changes.

9. Important Questions for Practice

1. Define reproduction. Why is it necessary?
Reproduction is the process by which living organisms produce new individuals of their own kind. It ensures continuity of a species.
2. Differentiate between asexual and sexual reproduction.
Asexual reproduction usually involves one parent and no fusion of gametes. Sexual reproduction involves gametes and their fusion during fertilisation.
3. Explain binary fission and budding with examples.
In binary fission, one cell divides into two (Amoeba). In budding, a bud develops on the parent (Yeast or Hydra).
4. What is fragmentation? Give an example.
The parent breaks into pieces and each suitable fragment can grow into a new individual. Example: Spirogyra.
5. What is vegetative propagation? Give two examples.
It is the formation of new plants from roots, stems or leaves. Examples: potato and ginger.
6. Name the male and female reproductive parts of a flower.
Stamen is the male part; pistil or carpel is the female part.
7. Differentiate between pollination and fertilisation.
Pollination is the transfer of pollen from anther to stigma. Fertilisation is the fusion of male and female gametes.
8. What happens to the ovule and ovary after fertilisation?
The ovule develops into a seed and the ovary usually develops into a fruit.
9. Define gamete and zygote.
A gamete is a reproductive cell. A zygote is the cell formed after fusion of gametes.
10. Why does sexual reproduction lead to variation?
Genetic material from two gametes combines and creates a new combination of hereditary information.

⚡ 30-Second Quick Revision

Reproduction maintains the continuity of a species. Asexual reproduction usually has one parent and no gamete fusion; sexual reproduction involves gametes and fertilisation. Asexual methods: binary fission, multiple fission, budding, fragmentation, regeneration, spore formation and vegetative propagation. In flowering plants, pollination is followed by fertilisation; the ovule becomes a seed and the ovary usually becomes a fruit. In humans, sperm and egg fuse to form a zygote.

Study tip: Practise a labelled flower diagram and revise the examples of each asexual reproduction method. Also learn the difference between pollination and fertilisation.
